"Furnished" means different things to different people. For a stay of a month or more, it should mean you can arrive with a suitcase and live normally from day one. Renters can use this checklist to ask the right questions, and owners can use it to set up a place renters will pick.

## Bedroom
- A bed with a mattress protector, sheets, pillows and blankets
- A dresser or closet space with hangers
- Blackout curtains or blinds
- A nightstand and a lamp

## Bathroom
- Towels and bath mats
- A hair dryer
- A shower curtain or door, and a plunger

## Kitchen
- Pots, pans, baking sheets
- Plates, bowls, glasses, mugs and utensils
- A coffee maker, toaster or toaster oven, and a microwave
- Knives, cutting boards and basic utensils
- Dish soap, a sponge and trash bags to get started

## Living and working
- A couch or comfortable seating
- A table where you can eat or work
- Reliable Wi-Fi, with the speed listed
- Enough outlets near the desk and the bed

## Home basics
- A washer and dryer in the unit, or shared laundry access
- A vacuum, a broom and cleaning supplies
- A smoke detector and a carbon monoxide detector
- A first-aid kit and a fire extinguisher
- Instructions for the thermostat, the trash days and the Wi-Fi password

## For owners
You don't need designer furniture. Clean, matching basics and good light matter more. Photograph every room, mention the Wi-Fi speed, and list exactly what's included, so renters don't have to ask.
